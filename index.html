Fundamental Principle

Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation

Published in Newton's famous treatise 'Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy' (Principia, 1687)

Gravitational Constant ($G$) $6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\text{kg}^{-2}$

Official NCERT Statement

"Every body in the universe attracts every other body with a force which is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them."

Magnitude of Gravitational Force NCERT Eq. (7.5)
$$|\mathbf{F}| = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$
$m_1, m_2$ Masses of point bodies ($\text{kg}$)
$r$ Distance between centers ($\text{m}$)

Important Applicability Note

Equation (7.5) strictly refers to point masses. For finite extended bodies, we must vectorially sum forces over all point-mass elements—though for spherically symmetric bodies of uniform density, the mass can be treated as concentrated at the geometric center.

Historical Reasoning & Derivation

The Falling Apple & The Orbiting Moon

How Isaac Newton deduced the Inverse-Square Law by comparing terrestrial gravity $g$ with lunar centripetal acceleration $a_m$

Unification of Physics Terrestrial + Celestial Mechanics
Step 01 NCERT Eq. (7.3)

Moon's Centripetal Acceleration

Newton reasoned that the Moon revolving in a nearly circular orbit of radius $R_m$ around Earth is subject to a centripetal acceleration $a_m$ due to Earth's gravity:

$$a_m = \frac{V^2}{R_m} = \frac{4\pi^2 R_m}{T^2}$$

where orbital speed $V$ is related to time period $T$ by the relation: $$V = \frac{2\pi R_m}{T}$$

Orbital Period ($T$):$27.3\text{ days} \approx 2.36 \times 10^6\text{ s}$
Lunar Orbit Radius ($R_m$):$3.84 \times 10^8\text{ m}$
Step 02 Distance Decay

Comparing $a_m$ with Surface Gravity $g$

Substituting known astronomical values into Equation (7.3) yields a value of $a_m$ much smaller than acceleration due to gravity $g$ at Earth's surface:

$$a_m = \frac{4 \times \pi^2 \times (3.84 \times 10^8\text{ m})}{(2.36 \times 10^6\text{ s})^2} \approx 0.00272\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

Meanwhile, at the surface of the Earth (distance $R_E \approx 6.4 \times 10^6\text{ m}$ from Earth's center): $$g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

This clearly demonstrates that the force due to Earth's gravity decreases rapidly with distance.
Step 03 NCERT Eq. (7.4)

The Famous $3600$ Ratio Verification

Assuming gravitational force decreases in proportion to the inverse square of distance from Earth's center ($a_m \propto R_m^{-2}$ and $g \propto R_E^{-2}$), we get:

$$\frac{g}{a_m} = \frac{R_m^2}{R_E^2} \approx \left(\frac{3.84 \times 10^8}{6.4 \times 10^6}\right)^2 = 60^2 = 3600$$

Taking the ratio of measured accelerations: $$\frac{9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}}{0.00272\text{ m s}^{-2}} \approx 3600$$

Exact agreement! This triumph led Newton to propose the Universal Law of Gravitation.

Vector Form & Principle of Superposition

Complete mathematical representation of NCERT Figures 7.3 & 7.4 with live interactive 2D vector sandbox

Newton's 3rd Law: $\mathbf{F}_{12} = -\mathbf{F}_{21}$

1. Vector Form of Newton's Law

Ref: NCERT Fig. 7.3

Let two point masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ be located at position vectors $\mathbf{r}_1$ and $\mathbf{r}_2$ relative to origin $O$. The displacement vector from $m_1$ to $m_2$ is: $$\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_1 \quad \text{and unit vector} \quad \hat{\mathbf{r}} = \frac{\mathbf{r}}{|\mathbf{r}|}$$

Force on point mass $m_2$ due to $m_1$: $$\mathbf{F} = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} (-\hat{\mathbf{r}}) = -G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}} = -G \frac{m_1 m_2}{|\mathbf{r}|^3} \mathbf{r}$$
  • Why the Negative Sign? Gravitational force is attractive, meaning the force $\mathbf{F}$ on $m_2$ is directed towards $m_1$ (along $-\mathbf{r}$).
  • Mutual Attraction: By Newton's Third Law, gravitational force $\mathbf{F}_{12}$ on body 1 due to 2 and $\mathbf{F}_{21}$ on body 2 due to 1 satisfy: $$\mathbf{F}_{12} = -\mathbf{F}_{21}$$

2. Principle of Superposition

Ref: NCERT Fig. 7.4

For a collection of point masses ($m_1, m_2, m_3, m_4, \dots$), the total gravitational force on any one mass (say $m_1$) is the vector sum of the individual gravitational forces exerted on it by all other point masses independently:

$$\mathbf{F}_1 = \frac{G m_2 m_1}{r_{21}^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{21} + \frac{G m_3 m_1}{r_{31}^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{31} + \frac{G m_4 m_1}{r_{41}^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{41}$$

Problem 01 • NCERT Ex 7.2(a)

Force at the Centroid of an Equilateral Triangle

Three equal masses of $m\text{ kg}$ each are fixed at the vertices $A, B,$ and $C$ of an equilateral triangle $ABC$. What is the net gravitational force acting on a mass $2m$ placed at the centroid $G$ of the triangle? Take $AG = BG = CG = 1\text{ m}$.

Step-by-Step Vector Resolution:

Choosing axes such that $A$ lies on the positive $Y$-axis ($\hat{\mathbf{j}}$), the angle between $GC$ and positive $X$-axis is $30^\circ$, and between $GB$ and negative $X$-axis is $30^\circ$. The individual forces on mass $2m$ at $G$ are:

$$\mathbf{F}_{GA} = \frac{G m (2m)}{1^2}\hat{\mathbf{j}} = 2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}}$$ $$\mathbf{F}_{GB} = 2Gm^2 \left(-\hat{\mathbf{i}}\cos 30^\circ - \hat{\mathbf{j}}\sin 30^\circ\right)$$ $$\mathbf{F}_{GC} = 2Gm^2 \left(+\hat{\mathbf{i}}\cos 30^\circ - \hat{\mathbf{j}}\sin 30^\circ\right)$$

By the Principle of Superposition, the resultant force $\mathbf{F}_R = \mathbf{F}_{GA} + \mathbf{F}_{GB} + \mathbf{F}_{GC}$:

$$\mathbf{F}_R = 2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}} + 2Gm^2\left(-2\hat{\mathbf{j}}\sin 30^\circ\right) = 2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}} - 2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}} = \mathbf{0}$$

Answer: Net gravitational force at the centroid is $\mathbf{0}\text{ N}$ (also obvious by symmetry!).

Problem 02 • NCERT Ex 7.2(b)

Symmetry Breaking by Doubling Vertex Mass

In the same equilateral triangle $ABC$ (with $AG = BG = CG = 1\text{ m}$ and mass $2m$ at centroid $G$), what is the net gravitational force on the centroid mass $2m$ if the mass at vertex $A$ is doubled (i.e., becomes $2m$)?

Step-by-Step Calculation:

Now the mass at vertex $A$ is $2m$. Thus, the upward force $\mathbf{F}_{GA}$ along $+\hat{\mathbf{j}}$ doubles:

$$\mathbf{F}'_{GA} = \frac{G (2m)(2m)}{1^2}\hat{\mathbf{j}} = 4Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}}$$

From Part (a), we already know that the horizontal components of $\mathbf{F}_{GB}$ and $\mathbf{F}_{GC}$ cancel out, and their vertical sum is:

$$\mathbf{F}_{GB} + \mathbf{F}_{GC} = -2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}}$$

Therefore, net resultant force $\mathbf{F}_R$ is:

$$\mathbf{F}_R = \mathbf{F}'_{GA} + \mathbf{F}_{GB} + \mathbf{F}_{GC} = 4Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}} - 2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}} = 2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}}$$

Answer: $\mathbf{F}_R = 2Gm^2\hat{\mathbf{j}}\text{ N}$ (directed along $GA$ towards vertex $A$).

Problem 03 • Vector Form

Gravitational Force in Cartesian Vector Notation

A point mass $m_1 = 100\text{ kg}$ is placed at position $\mathbf{r}_1 = (\hat{\mathbf{i}} + 2\hat{\mathbf{j}})\text{ m}$ and another point mass $m_2 = 200\text{ kg}$ is at $\mathbf{r}_2 = (4\hat{\mathbf{i}} + 6\hat{\mathbf{j}})\text{ m}$. Find the gravitational force vector $\mathbf{F}_{21}$ on $m_2$ due to $m_1$. ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\text{kg}^{-2}$).

Displacement vector from $m_1$ to $m_2$:

$$\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_1 = (4 - 1)\hat{\mathbf{i}} + (6 - 2)\hat{\mathbf{j}} = (3\hat{\mathbf{i}} + 4\hat{\mathbf{j}})\text{ m}$$

Magnitude of separation:

$$|\mathbf{r}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\text{ m}$$

Using NCERT vector equation $\mathbf{F}_{21} = -G \frac{m_1 m_2}{|\mathbf{r}|^3}\mathbf{r}$:

$$\mathbf{F}_{21} = -\frac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 100 \times 200}{5^3}(3\hat{\mathbf{i}} + 4\hat{\mathbf{j}}) = -1.067 \times 10^{-8}(3\hat{\mathbf{i}} + 4\hat{\mathbf{j}})\text{ N}$$

Answer: $\mathbf{F}_{21} = -(3.20\hat{\mathbf{i}} + 4.27\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \times 10^{-8}\text{ N}$.

Problem 04 • Equilibrium

Zero-Gravity (Null Point) Between Two Masses

Two spherical bodies of masses $M$ and $9M$ are separated by a center-to-center distance $d = 12\text{ m}$. At what distance $x$ from the smaller mass $M$ along the line joining them will a test mass $m_0$ experience zero net gravitational force?

For net gravitational force on $m_0$ to be zero, attractive pulls from $M$ and $9M$ must be equal and opposite:

$$G\frac{M m_0}{x^2} = G\frac{(9M) m_0}{(d - x)^2} \implies \frac{1}{x^2} = \frac{9}{(d - x)^2}$$

Taking positive square root (since $0 < x < d$):

$$\frac{1}{x} = \frac{3}{d - x} \implies d - x = 3x \implies 4x = d \implies x = \frac{d}{4}$$ $$x = \frac{12\text{ m}}{4} = 3.0\text{ m}$$

Answer: At $x = 3.0\text{ m}$ from mass $M$ (and $9.0\text{ m}$ from mass $9M$).

Problem 05 • Superposition

Four Identical Masses at the Corners of a Square

Four point masses each of mass $m$ are placed at the four corners of a square of side $a$. Find the magnitude of the resultant gravitational force on any one of the masses due to the remaining three masses.

Consider a corner mass $m$. The two adjacent masses at distance $a$ exert perpendicular forces of magnitude $F_1 = F_2 = \frac{Gm^2}{a^2}$.

Their resultant along the diagonal is:

$$F_{12} = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2} = \sqrt{2}\frac{Gm^2}{a^2}$$

The mass at the opposite diagonal corner is at distance $r_d = \sqrt{2}a$, exerting force along the same diagonal:

$$F_3 = \frac{Gm^2}{(\sqrt{2}a)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\frac{Gm^2}{a^2}$$

Total force along the diagonal towards the center of the square:

$$F_{\text{net}} = F_{12} + F_3 = \left(\sqrt{2} + \frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{Gm^2}{a^2} = \left(\frac{2\sqrt{2} + 1}{2}\right)\frac{Gm^2}{a^2}$$

Answer: $F_{\text{net}} = \left(\frac{2\sqrt{2} + 1}{2}\right)\frac{Gm^2}{a^2} \approx 1.914\frac{Gm^2}{a^2}$ directed towards the center.

Problem 06 • HOTS

Condition for Maximum Gravitational Attraction

A total mass $M$ is split into two parts $m$ and $(M - m)$, which are then separated by a fixed distance $r$. For what ratio $\frac{m}{M}$ will the gravitational force of attraction between the two parts be maximum?

Gravitational force between the two parts at fixed distance $r$ is:

$$F(m) = \frac{G}{r^2} m(M - m) = \frac{G}{r^2}(Mm - m^2)$$

For $F$ to be maximum, differentiate $F$ with respect to $m$ and equate to zero:

$$\frac{dF}{dm} = \frac{G}{r^2}(M - 2m) = 0 \implies M - 2m = 0 \implies \frac{m}{M} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Answer: $\frac{m}{M} = \frac{1}{2}$ (force is maximum when mass is divided into two equal halves).
